The complete insurance discount menu: 23 discounts your carrier offers but won't tell you about

Carriers maintain a full discount menu but aren't required to apply discounts you don't ask for. Most people are missing at least four. Here's the complete list.

Insurance carriers maintain what's sometimes called a "discount menu" — a full list of price reductions you can qualify for. The problem is they're under no obligation to apply discounts you don't ask for, and their agents aren't always incentivized to volunteer them. Discounts reduce premium revenue. Asking for them is your job.

This is the complete list. Most people are missing at least four.

Auto insurance discounts

Bundling discounts

Multi-policy (home + auto): The single largest discount available to most people. Combining home and auto insurance with the same carrier typically saves 10–18% on both policies. On a $1,400 auto premium and a $1,800 home premium, that's $580/year before you negotiate anything else.

Multi-vehicle: Two or more cars on the same policy. Usually 8–12% per additional vehicle. Worth asking about even if the second driver is an adult child — call and ask whether your state allows it.

Life insurance bundle: Fewer carriers offer this, but some (Erie, Amica, State Farm) discount P&C premiums when you hold a life policy with them.

Vehicle and driving discounts

Safe driver / accident-free: At the 3-year and 5-year marks, most carriers add a specific discount tier. At USAA, five continuous claim-free years can mean 10–15% off. Ask specifically: "What is my current claims-free discount?"

Defensive driving course: A state-approved course — typically 4–6 hours, available online, costs $25–45 — qualifies you for a 5–10% discount that usually lasts 3 years. The ROI on a $35 course that saves $90/year for three years is extraordinary. Eligible at any age.

Good student: Full-time students with a GPA of 3.0 or higher qualify for 8–15% off. Applies until age 25 in most states. Often requires submitting a transcript annually.

Low mileage / pay-per-mile: If you drive under 7,500 miles per year, you likely qualify. Work from home? This almost certainly applies to you. Some carriers (Nationwide SmartMiles, Metromile) offer true pay-per-mile pricing that can cut premiums 20–40%.

Telematics / usage-based: Programs like Progressive Snapshot, State Farm Drive Safe & Save, and Allstate Drivewise track your driving via an app and price your premium based on actual behavior. Safe drivers typically save 10–30%.

Anti-lock brakes: Federal law required ABS on all cars after September 2000. If your car has it, you qualify — but it may not be on your policy. Ask.

Airbags: Front, side, and curtain airbags each have associated discounts at many carriers. List what your vehicle has and ask specifically.

Anti-theft device: Factory-installed systems (LoJack, OnStar) or aftermarket devices both qualify. Discount ranges from 2–15%.

New car discount: Vehicles less than 3 years old qualify for a small discount at many carriers, based on the assumption that newer cars have better safety systems.

Lifestyle and affiliation discounts

Military / veterans: USAA is the gold standard, but if you're not eligible for USAA, many other carriers offer military discounts. Geico has long offered active-duty rates. Don't assume your carrier knows about your service history.

Federal employee: Geico's original name was Government Employees Insurance Company. Federal employees and family members often qualify for preferred rates.

Employer group / alumni association: Dozens of major carriers participate in group discount programs through employers, credit unions, and alumni associations. Your HR department or alumni office may have a partnership you don't know about.

Professional organizations: Engineers, teachers, nurses, and members of AAA or AARP often have access to negotiated group rates. AAA membership ($60–80/year) can pay for itself in insurance discounts alone.

Homeowner discount on auto: Simply owning your home can earn a small auto discount at some carriers — you're statistically a lower risk, even if you don't bundle.

Homeowners insurance discounts

Home alarm monitoring: A monitored burglar alarm system earns 5–15% off homeowners premiums. A local alarm (siren only) earns less. Smoke detectors: 2–5%. Carbon monoxide detectors: 2–4%. Ask for each separately.

New home / recent construction: Homes under 10 years old typically qualify. Recently renovated electrical, plumbing, or roofing systems can earn similar credits — document them.

Roof age and material: Impact-resistant roofing (Class 4) can save 20–30% in hail-prone states. In Florida, wind mitigation inspections can reduce wind coverage premiums by 20–50%. If you're in Florida and haven't had one, schedule one now.

Green home features: Solar panels, certain insulation upgrades, and Energy Star appliances qualify for discounts at a growing number of carriers.

Payment and administrative discounts

Paid-in-full: Paying your annual premium in a single payment saves 5–10% at most carriers. On a $1,800 homeowners policy, that's $90–180 for a simple behavioral change.

Autopay: Usually 3–7% off. Stacks with paid-in-full at some carriers.

Paperless billing: Small (2–5%) but requires zero effort. Switch to paperless in the same call.

Early renewal: Some carriers offer a small credit if you bind the new policy 7–10 days before your old one expires. Ask at renewal time.

The discount audit call: the exact words to use

Say this when you call: "I'd like to do a full discount review on my policy. Can you go through the complete discount menu for my state and tell me which ones are currently applied and which ones I might qualify for that aren't?"

The phrase "complete discount menu" signals that you know the concept exists and aren't going to accept a partial answer. Most customer service reps are trained to respond to it. The call takes 15–20 minutes and the median savings for people who haven't done it in 3+ years is $200–400/year.

Discounts that are sometimes traps

Telematics for aggressive drivers: If you brake hard, accelerate fast, or drive late at night, a telematics program can increase your premium. Try it for one period and compare before committing.

Disappearing deductible: Some carriers offer "vanishing deductible" programs where your deductible drops $50–100/year you're claim-free, but charge a fee to participate. Run the math on whether the fee exceeds the deductible reduction.

Bundling with a weak carrier: Bundling is the right idea, but not if it means moving your home policy to a carrier with a poor claims record. The discount doesn't compensate for a carrier that will fight your roof claim.
